LUAZ Wellness — Website Design Brief
For Figma (AI generation or a designer) — content, structure, style & references, ready to hand off
Prepared for NB · August 2026
How to Use This
This brief has everything a designer — or Figma's AI generation tools (First Draft / Make, or a plugin like Galileo/Uizard if you use those instead) — needs to design the site without guessing. There are three parts: a condensed prompt you can paste directly into an AI design tool as a starting point, the full section-by-section content and layout order for a human designer (or for refining the AI's first draft), and a curated set of reference sites with notes on specifically what to borrow from each.
1. Quick-Paste Prompt (for Figma AI / First Draft)
Design a clean, modern B2B website for "LUAZ Wellness," a German company that brings certified onsite/offsite yoga, fitness and nutrition sessions to corporate teams — and handles all the legal paperwork so the sessions qualify for Germany's tax-free employee wellness budget. Primary audience: HR/People teams at German SMEs (50–500 employees). Style: light, airy, minimal text, generous white space, images/illustration doing more work than words. Color: you choose — pick a light, modern, low-saturation accent color and tone (warm or cool, your call) on a white/cream base; the only hard rule is no dark or near-black background sections anywhere on the site. Rounded corners, soft shadows, simple line-art or abstract illustrations instead of stock photos. Structure: sticky header with logo + nav (How it works / For HR / For Instructors / Personalized Gifting) + a prominent "Book a 15-min call" pill button; hero with short punchy headline and one CTA; a 3-stat trust strip; a 3-step "how it works"; a two-card section contrasting "the experience" and "the compliance"; a dedicated For HR section (single contract, one invoice, compliance report per session); an onsite-vs-offsite section; a certified-instructors badge row; a For Instructors section; a Personalized Gifting section with 4 occasion cards (each up to €50, tax-free, curated to the person) plus an optional handwritten-note add-on; a bold final CTA band; simple footer. Tone: short, confident, benefit-led sentences — never more than one sentence per card. Reference the visual polish of Ramp.com and Cobee.io, the whitespace/typography discipline of Linear.app (but in light mode, not dark), and the section rhythm of TeamOut.com.
2. Brand Basics
●	Name: LUAZ Wellness
●	One-line positioning: Onsite & offsite wellness for German teams — compliant by design.
●	Primary audience: HR / People teams at German SMEs (50–500 employees)
●	Secondary audiences (their own section, not the main narrative): freelance yoga/fitness/nutrition instructors looking for corporate work; employees receiving personalized gifts
●	Voice: confident, warm, plain-spoken — no corporate jargon, no long paragraphs. One idea per sentence.
3. Color Palette — Left Open on Purpose
Rather than locking in a hex code, leave the exact color choice to Figma / the designer. Give them the brief below instead of swatches:
●	Base: white or cream background — light and airy, not stark-white clinical.
●	Accent: one confident, light-to-medium tone color of the designer's choosing (warm like amber/coral, or cool like sage/lavender — either direction works for this brand). Used for buttons, highlights, key numbers, icons and one or two "pop" blocks.
●	Hard rule — non-negotiable: no dark or near-black background fills anywhere on the site. Where a design would normally reach for a dark "contrast" block (as Linear or Ramp sometimes do), use a deeper/more saturated shade of the same light accent color instead — never black or charcoal.
●	Text: a dark warm-grey or near-black is fine for text only — never as a background fill.
This was tested with two candidate palettes already (a warm yellow, and a soft lavender) and both worked structurally — so the layout and content below aren't dependent on which direction the color goes. Let Figma propose 2–3 palette options against this brief and pick the one that reads cleanest.

5. Imagery & Illustration Direction
Across the whole site: favor an image or illustration over a paragraph wherever the two could carry the same idea. Every section above is written as one short sentence plus a visual, not a paragraph — keep it that way through design.
●	No stock photos of generic “office people.” The brand has no real photography yet, and fake-looking stock photos undercut trust more than they help — use abstract line-art illustrations, simple geometric shapes, or soft blob backgrounds instead.
●	Small “floating card” UI elements near the hero illustration (e.g. a card reading “Compliance file attached”) are a nice, cheap way to visually hint at the product without needing real screenshots yet.
●	Give the three secondary sections (For HR, For Instructors, Personalized Gifting) each a small distinct icon or illustration in their panel — makes them scannable at a glance instead of reading as three blocks of similar text.
●	Once real session photos or instructor headshots exist, they can replace the illustrations — but don't fake it in the meantime.
6. Reference Websites
Site	Link	What to borrow from it
TeamOut	teamout.com	Section rhythm and pacing — short hero, trust logos, icon-based service cards, a clear numbered “how it works,” CTA repeated after every major section rather than just once.
Cobee	cobee.io/en	How to talk to an HR buyer specifically — benefit-catalog icon cards, short ROI-flavored one-liners, tabbed feature breakdowns, “Request a demo” placed everywhere without feeling pushy.
Ramp	ramp.com	Clean, light, high-trust B2B layout — bold single-line hero tagline, strong social-proof block (logos + a big stat + short testimonials), confident whitespace.
Linear	linear.app	Typography and whitespace discipline, and how to visualize a process/workflow instead of just listing features. Note: Linear itself is dark-themed — borrow the spacing and type hierarchy only, not the color scheme.
YuLife	yulife.com	Closest direct analog — an HR-benefits brand built entirely around employee engagement and a distinctive single accent color used with real conviction, which is the same move LUAZ is making with yellow.
